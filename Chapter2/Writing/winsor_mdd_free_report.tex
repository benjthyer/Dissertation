\begin{abstract}
We invert the thermal history of Win-06-01b goethite from the water-saturated
step-heating experiment HT-Win-06-01b in two stages. First, multi-domain
diffusion (MDD) models of $N = 1$--\MDDFreeNmax{} spherical domains, with all
$^3$He fractions and sizes free, are fitted to the $^3$He release alone
\citep{farleyflowers2012}. The domain structure stabilizes at $N=4$: 97\% of the $^3$He resides in
one domain slightly larger than the Stage~1 reference domain ($r/r_0=1.12$),
and three small domains holding 2.8\% account for the high early diffusivities.
Second, with that structure fixed, thermal histories are fitted to the
bulk-normalized $^4$He/$^3$He spectrum and checked against the (U--Th)/He age
of 130--180~Ma. Steady linear cooling to 25\,\textdegree C fits best
($\chi^2_\nu=2.0$ for paths consistent with the He age): crystallization at
150--175~Ma at 76--80\,\textdegree C. A burial-and-exhumation path with a Late
Cretaceous peak of 58--65\,\textdegree C fits less well ($\chi^2_\nu\approx4.8$--5.1)
but agrees with regional apatite fission-track constraints. An isothermal hold
fits poorly ($\chi^2_\nu\approx18$). The cooling solution favours crystallization
from warm (hydrothermal) fluids over formation at the surface.
\end{abstract}

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation and data}

Win-06-01b is dense colloform goethite collected as float in the Flinders
Ranges, South Australia, with a (U--Th)/He age of 130--180~Ma
\citep{waltenberg2012}. Apatite fission-track studies in the region record
cooling from roughly 50--70\,\textdegree C after the mid-Cretaceous
\citep{gibsonstuwe2000,mitchell1998}, and Triassic intramontane basins less
than 1.5~km thick indicate that parts of the ranges were near the surface in the
Early Mesozoic \citep{mitchell1998}. A proton-irradiated aliquot was step-heated
in a sealed silica ampoule under water-saturated conditions (HT-Win-06-01b; 34
steps, 170--250\,\textdegree C), releasing 14.8\% of its $^3$He before final
fusion. The Stage~1 regression of that experiment defines the reference
Arrhenius line used here
($E_a=\ChTwoEa$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, $\ln(D_0/a_0^2)=16.16$ with $D_0/a_0^2$ in s$^{-1}$).

Earlier versions of this analysis split the sample into a high-retentivity
domain pinned to the Stage~1 line and a low-retentivity assemblage holding at most 7.5\% of the $^3$He. Here that distinction is dropped: all
domains are equivalent, and each has a free $^3$He fraction and size.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Domain model}
The sample is represented by $N$ non-interacting spherical domains that share
the Stage~1 activation energy and frequency factor. Domain $j$ holds a
fraction $f_j$ of the uniformly distributed, proton-induced $^3$He
($\sum_j f_j=1$) and has radius $r_j$ relative to the reference radius $r_0$
implied by the Stage~1 regression, so that
\begin{equation}
  \left(\frac{D}{a^2}\right)_j = \left(\frac{r_j}{r_0}\right)^{-2}\frac{D_0}{a_0^2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right).
  \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
A model with $N$ domains has $k=2N-1$ free parameters. Sizes are bounded by
$10^{-6}\le r_j/r_0\le10^{3}$.

\subsection{Laboratory release of $^3$He}
For a heating schedule of steps $i$ with temperatures $T_i$ and durations
$\Delta t_i$, the reference domain accumulates the non-dimensional time
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{0,i} = \sum_{m\le i}\frac{D_0}{a_0^2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{RT_m}\right)\Delta t_m ,
  \label{eq:tau0}
\end{equation}
and domain $j$ reaches $\tau_{0,i}(r_j/r_0)^{-2}$. An initially uniform sphere
releases the cumulative fraction \citep{crank1979}
\begin{equation}
  F(\tau) = 1-\frac{6}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^2}\exp\!\left(-k^2\pi^2\tau\right),
  \label{eq:Fsphere}
\end{equation}
so the model cumulative and fractional $^3$He yields of the sample are
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma F_i = \sum_{j=1}^{N} f_j\,F\!\left(\tau_{0,i}\,(r_j/r_0)^{-2}\right),
  \qquad \Delta F_i = \Sigma F_i - \Sigma F_{i-1}.
  \label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fitting the domain structure to $^3$He}
Following \citet[supplement~A2]{farleyflowers2012}, the fractional and cumulative
yields are weighted equally, and $\{f_j, r_j\}$ minimize
\begin{equation}
  S = \sum_{i=1}^{34}\left(\Delta F_i^{\mathrm{mod}}-\Delta F_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)^2
    + \sum_{i=1}^{34}\left(\Sigma F_i^{\mathrm{mod}}-\Sigma F_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right)^2 ,
  \label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
with yields expressed as fractions of the total $^3$He, including the fusion
step. The six steps with less than 0.1~pccSTP of $^3$He, which are excluded
throughout the chapter, are left out of both sums (28 steps remain). Their gas
still counts toward the cumulative yields of later steps, and their durations
toward the laboratory schedule. Models are built up one domain at a time: the $N$-domain search starts from
the $(N-1)$-domain solution plus one new domain trialled at 15 sizes and two
fractions, together with ten random starts. The fit therefore cannot get worse
as $N$ increases. We add domains until a new domain no longer changes the
fit or the domain structure.
The Arrhenius and delta plots are not fitted. They follow from applying the
single-sphere inversion of \citet{fechtigkalbitzer1966} to the model
cumulative release. Their misfit is reported as a reduced $\chi^2$ against the
bounding-method uncertainties in $\ln(D/a^2)$ over the same 28 steps.

\subsection{Radiogenic $^4$He in nature}
Each domain produces $^4$He uniformly at rate $P$ and loses it by diffusion
with a zero-concentration boundary,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial C}{\partial t}=\frac{D}{a^2}\,\frac{1}{x^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(x^2\frac{\partial C}{\partial x}\right)+P,
  \qquad C(1,t)=0,\quad C(x,t_\mathrm{form})=0 ,
  \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
where $x$ is radial position normalized to the domain radius and
$t_\mathrm{form}$ is the crystallization age. With
$C=\sum_k A_k(t)\sin(k\pi x)/x$ \citep{carslawjaeger1959}, each mode evolves as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}A_k}{\mathrm{d}t}=-k^2\pi^2\frac{D}{a^2}A_k+c_kP,\qquad c_k=\frac{2(-1)^{k+1}}{k\pi},
  \label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
which over an interval $\Delta t$ (2~Myr) at constant temperature has the exact solution
\begin{equation}
  A_k \leftarrow A_k e^{-\lambda_k}+c_kP\,\Delta t\,\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_k}}{\lambda_k},
  \qquad \lambda_k=k^2\pi^2\frac{D}{a^2}\Delta t .
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
We use 8000 modes. The $^4$He content per unit volume of domain $j$ is
$N_j=3\sum_kA_{k,j}(-1)^{k+1}/(k\pi)$. Taking U and Th to be distributed like
the $^3$He and neglecting parent decay ($\sim$3\% over 200~Myr), the bulk He
age is
\begin{equation}
  t_\mathrm{He}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{j}f_j\,N_j .
  \label{eq:age}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Laboratory degassing of the natural profiles}
In the laboratory each mode decays as $\exp(-k^2\pi^2\tau)$. The reference
times $\tau_{0,i}$ used for the $^4$He prediction are solved from the
\emph{measured} cumulative $^3$He by inverting Eq.~\ref{eq:mixture}. The
predicted spectrum is therefore independent of the heating schedule and of
step-to-step kinetic scatter:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{R_{\mathrm{step},i}}{R_\mathrm{bulk}}=\frac{\Delta N_{4,i}/N_{4,\mathrm{tot}}}{\Delta F_i^{\mathrm{obs}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta N_{4,i}=\sum_jf_j\left[N_j(\tau_{0,i-1})-N_j(\tau_{0,i})\right] ,
  \label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
where $N_j(\tau)$ is the $^4$He remaining in domain $j$ after laboratory time
$\tau(r_j/r_0)^{-2}$ and $N_{4,\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_j f_jN_j(0)$ includes the
gas released at fusion, consistent with the measured bulk ratio.
Because the eigenfunctions do not depend on $D$, the present-day amplitudes
$A_{k,j}$ carry each domain's non-uniform $^4$He profile into the laboratory
unchanged. No assumption of a uniform $^4$He distribution is made. An
independent finite-volume solution reproduces the predicted spectrum to
$8\times10^{-4}$ in $R/R_\mathrm{bulk}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:check}).

\subsection{Thermal histories}
The thermal history is inverted with the domain structure fixed, so it must work
within the bounds set by the $^3$He inversion. We compare three families of
piecewise-linear thermal history, each with two free parameters.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Steady cooling:} $T$ falls linearly from $T_0$ at
  $t_\mathrm{form}$ to 25\,\textdegree C today. The goethite forms at an
  elevated temperature, for example from warm fluids at depth, and cools
  monotonically. Free parameters: $t_\mathrm{form}$ and $T_0$.
  \item \textbf{Burial and exhumation:} through $(t_\mathrm{form},T_s)$,
  $(t_\mathrm{peak},T_\mathrm{max})$, $(50~\mathrm{Ma},T_s)$ and $(0,T_s)$ with
  $T_s=20$\,\textdegree C. The goethite forms at the surface, is buried and
  heated to a peak temperature, and is exhumed by the Paleogene, when Paleogene
  sediments in Flinders Ranges valleys show that basement was exposed
  \citep{mitchell1998}. Free parameters: $t_\mathrm{peak}$ and $T_\mathrm{max}$,
  for fixed crystallization ages of 180, 195 and 210~Ma.
  \item \textbf{Isothermal hold:} $T=T_\mathrm{iso}$ from $t_\mathrm{form}$ to
  the present. Free parameters: $t_\mathrm{form}$ and $T_\mathrm{iso}$.
\end{itemize}
Each path is scored against
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2=\sum_i\left(\frac{R_i^{\mathrm{mod}}-R_i^{\mathrm{M}}}{\sigma_i}\right)^2,
  \qquad\sigma_i=\max\!\left[\tfrac12\left(R_i^{\mathrm{UB}}-R_i^{\mathrm{LB}}\right),\,0.02\right],
  \label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
over the \MDDNfit{} steps that have lag-correction bounds and more than
0.1~pccSTP of $^3$He, so $\nu=22$. M, LB and UB are the central, lower-bound and
upper-bound bulk-normalized ratios of the bounding method. $\chi^2$ is mapped on
regular grids:
\begin{itemize}
  \item burial: $t_\mathrm{peak}$ from 55~Ma to $t_\mathrm{form}-2.5$~Ma in 2.5~Myr steps, and $T_\mathrm{max}=30$--90\,\textdegree C in 1\,\textdegree C steps;
  \item steady cooling and isothermal hold: $t_\mathrm{form}=50$--500~Ma in 5~Myr steps, and $T_0=25$--120\,\textdegree C or $T_\mathrm{iso}=0$--70\,\textdegree C in 1\,\textdegree C steps.
\end{itemize}
Natural histories are integrated in 1--2~Myr intervals of constant temperature,
and the predicted He age is contoured at the limits of the measured range. The
joint 1$\sigma$ region of each map is
$\chi^2\le\chi^2_{\min}+2.30\max(1,\chi^2_\nu)$. We treat the He age as a hard
constraint. The best fit of each family is the lowest-$\chi^2$ path with a
predicted age of 130--180~Ma, and parameter ranges are those of the
age-consistent paths within the same $\Delta\chi^2$ of that best fit.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Domain structure from $^3$He}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} lists the fits for all $N$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:domains}
shows the fitted domain spectra.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$N=1$.} A single domain cannot fit the release (Arrhenius $\chi^2_\nu=27$).
  \item \textbf{$N=2$.} The second domain captures the high early diffusivities but leaves a poor Arrhenius fit ($\chi^2_\nu=3.3$).
  \item \textbf{$N=3$.} The yield misfit falls by more than half and the Arrhenius $\chi^2_\nu$ falls to 1.08.
  \item \textbf{$N=4$.} The smallest domain splits in two. The fit improves slightly further (Arrhenius $\chi^2_\nu=1.00$).
  \item \textbf{$N\ge5$.} Every added domain coincides in size with the dominant domain. The fit is unchanged.
\end{itemize}
We therefore adopt $N=4$ as the complete description of the $^3$He data. Its
domains are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 0.57\% of the $^3$He at $r/r_0=6.8\times10^{-4}$;
  \item 0.66\% at $2.9\times10^{-3}$;
  \item 1.5\% at 0.031;
  \item 97.2\% at 1.12.
\end{itemize}
The three small domains are fully degassed during the experiment. The dominant
domain releases 12\% of its $^3$He, at a diffusivity 0.80 times the Stage~1
line. The Stage~1 line is therefore a slightly fast description of the dominant
domain: it was regressed on bulk-inverted diffusivities that still carry a
small contribution from the fast domains. The model reproduces the Arrhenius
array, including the retrograde segments, and the gradual approach of the delta
plot to the Stage~1 line (Fig.~\ref{fig:arrhdelta}). Figure~\ref{fig:supp} shows the
fitted yields and the resulting Arrhenius and delta plots for $N=1$--5.

\paragraph{Steady cooling.} Linear cooling to 25\,\textdegree C fits the
spectrum best. The best age-consistent path starts at 78\,\textdegree C at 150~Ma
($\chi^2_\nu=2.00$), and the age-consistent 1$\sigma$ range is crystallization
at 150--175~Ma at 76--80\,\textdegree C, cooling at
$\sim$0.3\,\textdegree C\,Myr$^{-1}$. This path passes through
$\sim$55\,\textdegree C at 90~Ma, inside the apatite fission-track window. The
spectrum alone prefers somewhat younger, hotter paths: the unconstrained minimum
($\chi^2_\nu=\MDDLinGlobalChi$; \MDDLinGlobalT\,\textdegree C at
\MDDLinGlobalTform~Ma) predicts a He age of \MDDLinGlobalAge~Ma, and the
unconstrained 1$\sigma$ region lies entirely at ages younger than 130~Ma
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmaps}b). Requiring the measured age costs
$\Delta\chi^2\approx7$.

\paragraph{Burial and exhumation.} Burial paths fit the spectrum less well
($\chi^2_\nu=4.8$--6.2), but they agree with independent regional constraints. For
crystallization at 180 and 195~Ma, the best age-consistent paths peak at
61\,\textdegree C at 70 and 80~Ma, with 1$\sigma$ ranges of 58--65\,\textdegree C
between 98 and 60~Ma (Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmaps}a). These peaks fall in or just after
the apatite fission-track window and within the 50--70\,\textdegree C range of
\citet{gibsonstuwe2000}, at the $<$60\,\textdegree C limit reported near Copley
\citep{mitchell1998}. For crystallization at 210~Ma, only hotter and earlier
peaks (70--74\,\textdegree C at 105--130~Ma) give an age within the measured range,
and they fit worse ($\chi^2_\nu=6.2$).

\paragraph{Isothermal hold.} The spectrum constrains only the product of
diffusivity and time, so the $\chi^2$ minimum is a ridge from
$\sim$47\,\textdegree C at 50~Ma to $\sim$35\,\textdegree C at 500~Ma along which
$\chi^2_\nu\approx\MDDIsoChi$ throughout. No isothermal path reproduces the shape
of the spectrum: they underpredict the ratios of the early steps and overpredict
those of the late steps. We do not consider this family further.

\paragraph{Where the families differ.} The steady-cooling and burial
predictions differ mainly in the earliest steps
($\Sigma F_{^3\mathrm{He}}\lesssim0.035$; Fig.~\ref{fig:compare}). These steps sample
the outer rim of the dominant domain. On the burial path, the sample sits at
20\,\textdegree C for the last 50~Myr, long enough for radiogenic $^4$He to
re-accumulate in the rim, so the early ratios are overpredicted. Steady cooling
keeps the rim depleted until geologically recent times, with diffusive loss
declining monotonically toward the present. Elsewhere both leave the same
residual pattern: ratios underpredicted between $\Sigma F_{^3\mathrm{He}}=0.04$
and 0.06, and overpredicted in the two longest high-temperature steps.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Robust conclusions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The $^3$He release is described completely by four domains. The dominant domain holds $\approx$97\% of the $^3$He and degasses slightly more slowly than the Stage~1 line. Fast domains hold only 2.8\%.
  \item Within that structure, the spectrum requires a $^4$He-depleted rim in the dominant domain. The rim reflects diffusive loss at temperatures well above the surface during the Mesozoic, followed by loss that declined toward the present rather than stopping early.
  \item Both the steady-cooling and the burial solutions place the sample at $\sim$55--65\,\textdegree C in the Late Cretaceous, consistent with the apatite fission-track record of 50--70\,\textdegree C before cooling after $\sim$85~Ma \citep{gibsonstuwe2000}.
  \item Both require crystallization in the Jurassic or earliest Cretaceous: at 150--175~Ma for steady cooling, and at or after $\sim$195~Ma for burial.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Formation setting.} The two families encode different formation
settings. Burial assumes the goethite formed at the surface, for example in a
weathering profile, and was later buried. Steady cooling has it forming at
$\sim$76--80\,\textdegree C. The better fit of steady cooling therefore favours
crystallization from warm fluids, in a hydrothermal system or at depth, over
formation at the surface. Burial nevertheless remains a viable alternative: it is
the setting assumed by most goethite (U--Th)/He studies, and it matches the
independent fission-track constraints closely. Two further qualifications apply.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A hydrothermal system cools to ambient temperature in far less than a million years, so the 150~Myr linear decline is not the cooling of the system itself. It implies that the sample stayed in a warm subsurface setting after crystallization and cooled slowly, by exhumation or a declining geotherm. The two-parameter family cannot separate the fluid temperature at crystallization from the ambient temperature shortly afterwards.
  \item The comparison is between parameterized families. A burial path with a warmer Cenozoic, or a brief hydrothermal pulse followed by slow cooling, has not been tested and might fit as well.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Remaining misfit.} Even the steady-cooling solution leaves
systematic residuals. The intermediate domain ($r/r_0\approx0.03$, 1.5\% of the
$^3$He) degasses at $\Sigma F_{^3\mathrm{He}}\approx0.02$--0.06. With the common
activation energy, it would have lost most of its radiogenic $^4$He in nature,
which depresses the predicted ratios where the measured ratios are highest.
Diffusion-only remedies that preserve the $^3$He fit are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a higher activation energy for the intermediate domain, which the retrograde segments of the experiment could test;
  \item domain-dependent $^4$He production, for example from U--Th zoning across the colloform bands.
\end{itemize}
Each adds one parameter. A two-domain structure fits the burial spectrum
better ($\chi^2_\nu\approx1.3$) but reproduces the $^3$He Arrhenius array poorly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:supp}), so we do not adopt it.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The temperature scale rests on extrapolating the Stage~1 activation energy from 200--250\,\textdegree C to near-surface temperatures ($\pm\sim$4\,\textdegree C near 60\,\textdegree C, not propagated here).
  \item Surface temperatures (20\,\textdegree C for burial, 25\,\textdegree C for steady cooling) and the burial exhumation time (50~Ma) are fixed.
  \item Domains that barely degas in the experiment are constrained only through their summed fraction.
  \item The model takes U and Th to be distributed like the $^3$He, both within and among domains.
\end{itemize}
