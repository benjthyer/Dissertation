The Rustenburg Layered Series of the Bushveld Complex in the Mpumalanga, Limpopo, 
and Gauteng provinces of South Africa is amongst the 
world's best examples of a large, layered mafic-ultramafic intrusion 
and has been the focus of decades of research, yet the tempo and the fundamental 
nature of its formation remain open questions. Field research at key localities in 
the Bushveld Complex has provided invaluable insights into the emplacement and evolution of these layered intrusions,
yet the intrusion is vast; many areas with good exposure remain poorly studied. 
Draaikraal is an agricultural town located
in the eastern limb of the Bushveld Complex in the Mpumalanga province that sits at the platinum-rich intersection of the Critical 
and Main Zones with the intrusion's margin, presenting a valuable opportunity to study the formation geometry and timing of large mafic intrusions in general, with
particular emphasis on its famous Platinum Group Element (PGE) mineralization. 

Draaikraal is nested within a conspicuously recessed valley capped by ironstone laterites 
and hosts several unusual topographic and structural features, 
including the Kliprivier Synform and the Draaikraal Lineament (named here).
The Kliprivier Synform represents a package of modestly rotated, locally pegmatitic
plutonic rocks with conspicuously absent modal layering, and country rocks that
include fine-grained, melanocratic Marginal Zone sills intercalated with migmatized quartzite and calc-silicate hornfels. 
The country rocks appear to be displaced over 1 km into the plutonic RLS near the mapped intersection of the Critical
and Main Zones. The Draaikraal Lineament is a linear
feature of recessive topography stretching from the country rocks beyond
the intrusion's margin to the middle Main Zone. It overlaps with the
Kliprivier Synform and separates typical, resistant Main Zone
gabbronorites to the north from the recessive Main Zone gabbronorites to
the south in the agricultural valley hosting Draaikraal. Using structural
measurements and correlation of mottled anorthosite marker beds of the
lower Main Zone across the Draaikraal Lineament, I argue that the mottled anorthosite marker beds are
not displaced across the Draaikraal Lineament as previous studies have suggested. I show that the Kliprivier
Synform was deformed synmagmatically by Bushveld magmatism, and argue that it represents a monocline that formed due to laccolithic
deformation of the country rock roof by the intrusion of middle Critical Zone
sills below the level of the UG2 chromitite and overlying Merensky
Reef and Main Zones, which appear to be undisturbed. The orientation of the Draaikraal Lineament is very
similar to the surface-projection of the axial plane of the Kliprivier
Synform, which likely continues at depth. The relative recessive
topography of the Draaikraal Lineament and the Main Zone to the south is
argued to be the result of higher degrees of contamination by
hydrothermal fluids than the more resistant Main Zone gabbronorites of
the surrounding area. The rocks south of the Draaikraal Lineament are
envisaged to be fluxed with upwardly migrating fluids derived from devolatilization 
of underlying, relatively shallow sediments at the floor of the magma chamber whereas
the resistant Main Zone to the north was underlain by thick Critical Zone rocks and thus 
was less contaminated by crustal fluids.
